## LinkedIn: educational posts, English

### LinkedIn post A — Offline finance reporting

**A finance dashboard doesn't replace reconciliation. It makes reconciled data easier to act on.**

Picture a business owner reviewing sales, expenses, overdue receivables and budget variance from one collection of CSV exports. A local dashboard can make differences visible quickly—but only if the source data agrees with the actual books.

We documented how a single-user HTML dashboard works, how to prepare your imports, and the limits to keep in mind.

Read the practical guide: https://ribhlab.github.io/en/finance/offline-profit-dashboard.html

Our company sells the software described in this guide. #SmallBusinessFinance #FinancialReporting #CashFlow

### LinkedIn post B — Self-hosted team reporting

**When should a finance team move from one analyst's file to a shared reporting workspace?**

Three signs: several colleagues need access to the same dataset; import responsibilities need to be separated from read-only review; and backups/accountability need a named owner.

The trade-off: a self-hosted Python/SQLite tool requires maintenance, HTTPS and careful access control. It's not a managed cloud subscription.

Here's our breakdown of roles, deployment steps and limits: https://ribhlab.github.io/en/finance/self-hosted-team-finance-dashboard.html

RibhLabDigital is the seller. #FinanceTeams #BusinessReporting #SelfHosted

### LinkedIn post C — Choosing the right edition

**A dashboard should fit your workflow, not the other way around.**

Single user working locally? A browser-based offline dashboard may be sufficient.
Staff need shared access? A self-hosted Team tool can be more appropriate—if you can maintain it.
Need both for separate purposes? Compare a bundle, but don't assume the two databases sync.

A short practical comparison: https://ribhlab.github.io/en/finance/choose-finance-dashboard.html
